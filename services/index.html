Straight Talk

What usually goes wrong.

After a few thousand service calls, the list of failure causes gets short and familiar.

Broken springs are the number one reason a door suddenly will not move. You often hear a bang from the garage, and the opener will hum without lifting anything. That is not an opener problem, even though it looks like one.

Next come cables, then worn rollers and hinges, then the opener itself. A door that grinds, jerks or hangs crooked is usually telling you a mechanical part is on its way out, and the smart move is to deal with it before it snaps and bends a track.

If you are not sure which bucket your problem is in, call us. Describing the noise and the symptoms over the phone tells us a lot about what to bring.

Springs & Cables

The parts doing the heavy lifting.

A typical double door weighs well over a hundred pounds. The springs carry almost all of it, so your opener does not have to.

When a spring lets go, the door becomes very heavy and the opener cannot move it. That is why a broken spring stops everything. Spring work also carries real danger, because the tension is stored in the metal, so it is not a DIY job.

Torsion springs

These sit on a bar above the door. We replace them as a matched pair when the second one has done the same number of cycles, and we can fit high-cycle springs if your door opens a lot.

Extension springs

Found on many older doors. They run along the sides of the tracks, and we replace them together with their safety cables so a failed spring cannot whip across the garage.

Cables

Cables work with the springs. A frayed or off-drum cable leaves the door hanging crooked, and that is when tracks get bent, so it is worth fixing early.

Our spring work carries a one-year warranty.

Openers

Opener repair & LiftMaster installation.

If your opener hums, grinds or reverses for no reason, the door is often the real problem.

A worn spring or a dragging track makes a perfectly good opener look broken. We check the door first, then the opener, so you are not paying for a new motor you did not need.

New Doors

New garage door installation & replacement.

A new garage door changes the whole front of a house. It is also a door you will use twice a day for the next fifteen years.

We handle the entire job: measuring the opening, removing the old door, fitting the new panels, tracks, springs and hardware, and setting up the opener. Nothing is left to chance on sizing, because a door that is slightly out is a door that will give you trouble later.

Door styles and materials

  • Steel: tough and low maintenance, a solid all-round pick for our climate
  • Insulated: worth it if the garage is attached, has a room above it or doubles as a workshop
  • Wood and carriage house: classic looks, more upkeep in the rain
  • Aluminum and glass: modern and bright, popular on newer builds

Repair or replace?

If the door is only a few years old and the fault is a spring, cable or roller, repairing is almost always better value. If several panels are damaged, the door is rusting or warping, or parts are getting hard to find, replacement starts to make sense. We will give you an honest read either way, even if that means talking you out of a sale.

Common Questions

Garage door repair & installation FAQs.

Still unsure? Call 604-388-9868 and we will point you the right way.

How do I know if my garage door spring is broken?

You might hear a loud bang from the garage, the door will not lift or only rises a few inches, or you can see a gap in the spring. The opener may strain or hum without moving the door. Stop using it and call a technician.

How often should I have my garage door serviced?

Once a year is a good rule for most homes. A yearly tune-up catches worn rollers, tired springs, frayed cables and balance problems before they turn into a breakdown.

Should I repair or replace my garage door?

Repair is usually the better value when the door is fairly new and one part has failed, such as a spring, cable or roller. Replacement makes sense when several panels are damaged or the door is rusting, warping or hard to find parts for.

Do you install LiftMaster openers?
