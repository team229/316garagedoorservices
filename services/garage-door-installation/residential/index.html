Door Options

Which door fits your garage?

The right door depends on how you actually use the garage. A double bay with a workshop behind it needs different things than a single garage that only parks the car.

Steel

The workhorse. Steel handles our rain well, needs very little upkeep and comes in plenty of panel styles and colours. For most detached garages it is the sensible default.

Insulated

If the garage is attached, has a room above it, or you use it as a workshop, insulation earns its money back in comfort and noise. It also stops the door sounding like a drum.

Wood and carriage house

A wood or carriage-style door gives a house real character. These doors want more care in damp weather, so we are upfront about sealing and maintenance before you commit.

Aluminum and glass

Full-view doors suit modern homes and garages used as a studio or entertaining space. We can talk through privacy and tint options if that is a concern.

Single and double doors

We install both, and we measure the opening, headroom and side room first, because a door that is a hair too big is a door that will never run right.

Openers

A new door is a good time to upgrade the opener. We install LiftMaster and myQ units, including Wi-Fi models, and balance the door so the opener is not doing more work than it should.

Repair or Replace

Is it time for a new door?

Not every problem means a new door. A broken spring, frayed cable, worn roller or misaligned sensor is usually a repair.

Signs it is time to replace

  • Several panels are dented, cracked or separating
  • The door is rusting, warping or swelling from moisture
  • It goes out of balance again soon after repairs
  • Parts are getting hard to find for an older model
  • The door is noisy, drafty or poorly insulated
  • You want better security, insulation or curb appeal

Why pouring money into a worn door rarely pays off

On an old door, one repair leads to the next. Springs, rollers and hinges wear at similar rates, so fixing one part puts more strain on the others. A new door with new springs and hardware resets the clock and stops the repeat service calls. If you are unsure which way to go, call us. We will inspect the door, tell you what we find and give you both options with prices.

When is the best time to replace?

When the problems start, not when the door fails completely. A door that dies during the morning rush, or leaves the garage open overnight, turns a planned upgrade into an emergency. We install year-round, and planning ahead lets you choose the door you actually want instead of whatever is quickest. If you are renovating, selling, or just tired of how the front of the house looks, the garage door is a big part of that first impression.

Common Questions

Residential garage door installation FAQs.

Still unsure? Call 604-388-9868 and we will point you the right way.

How long does a residential garage door installation take?

Most single and double door installs take a few hours to a day, depending on the door, the condition of the old tracks and whether we are also installing an opener. We give you a time estimate at the consultation.

Can you install a new door and opener at the same time?

Yes, and it is often the best moment to do it. We install LiftMaster and myQ openers and balance the door so the opener lasts longer.

Do you remove and dispose of my old garage door?

Yes. We remove the old door, springs and hardware as part of the job. Ask us to confirm this when you get your quote.

Is an insulated garage door worth it?

It is worth considering if your garage is attached, has a room above it or is used as a workshop. Insulation helps with temperature and noise. For a detached garage that only holds the car, a standard steel door may be plenty.

Do I get a price before work starts, and is there a warranty?

Yes. Every call starts with a free consultation and a price you approve before work begins. Springs come with a one-year warranty, and there are no travel fees.

What are your hours for residential installations?
