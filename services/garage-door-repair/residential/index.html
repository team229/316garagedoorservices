What We Repair

The repairs your door is most likely to need.

Houses are different, but garage door failures are not. After thousands of calls, the same handful of problems come up again and again.

Broken springs

Springs carry the weight of the door. A residential door is easily over a hundred pounds, and without a working spring the opener is trying to lift all of it. When a spring breaks you usually hear a bang, and the door simply will not open. Please do not try to swap a spring yourself. The stored tension can hurt you badly.

  • Torsion springs sit on a bar above the door. We replace them as a matched set where it makes sense, because the spring that is still intact has done the same amount of work as the one that broke.
  • Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks and are common on older doors. We replace them with safety cables so a snapped spring cannot fly.

Spring work comes with a one-year warranty.

Cables

Cables work hand in hand with the springs, and a frayed or popped cable is what makes a door hang crooked. If you notice that, stop running the door. A crooked door bends tracks, and that turns a small repair into a bigger one.

Openers

When the opener hums but nothing moves, or a remote suddenly stops working, it is usually something small: a worn gear, a board fault, a dead battery or a programming issue. We repair LiftMaster and myQ units and other common brands, and we will be honest when a replacement is the better spend.

Sensors and weather seals

If the door reverses halfway down, the safety sensors are usually dirty or knocked out of line. Bottom seals and weather stripping wear out quietly, letting in drafts, water and pests. Both are quick, affordable fixes.

Off-track doors, rollers, hinges and panels

A door that has jumped the track, a noisy roller, a cracked hinge or a dented panel all get a proper look. We fix what can be fixed and replace what should be replaced.

Warning Signs

Sounds and signs worth paying attention to.

Doors rarely fail without warning. The warning is usually noise, movement or a slow-down.

  • Grinding, banging or squealing when the door moves
  • A door that is slow, jerky or uneven on one side
  • The door reversing on its own or refusing to close
  • A visible gap in a spring, or a frayed cable
  • A door that feels heavy when you lift it by hand
  • The opener running but the door not moving

Why it pays to act early

A worn spring or a frayed cable does not get better on its own. Running the door anyway can bend tracks, crack panels and burn out the opener. We would rather fix one part now than four parts later, and your wallet would too.

Maintenance

Tune-ups and the repair-or-replace call.

A yearly visit prevents a good chunk of the repairs above.

A tune-up means lubrication, tightening hardware, inspecting springs and cables, checking sensors and testing balance. In our wet Lower Mainland climate, moisture speeds up wear on rollers and hardware, so an annual check is genuinely worth it. It is also the cheapest insurance against a door that quits on a cold morning.

Repair or replace?

Some doors are worth repairing for years. Others cost more in repeat repairs than a new door would. We will give you a straight answer. If replacement is the better route, we also install new residential garage doors and openers.

Common Questions

Residential garage door repair FAQs.

Still unsure? Call 604-388-9868 and we will point you the right way.

How much does residential garage door repair cost?

Most repairs run about $25 to $50 for the work itself, depending on the project, with parts extra. A sensor adjustment costs far less than a spring replacement. We give you a free consultation and a price you approve before any work begins.

Why won't my garage door open?

The most common causes are a broken spring, a snapped cable, a dead remote battery, a misaligned sensor or an opener fault. Check that the door is not locked and the opener has power, then call us if it still will not move.

Is it safe to open my garage door with a broken spring?

We do not recommend it. Without spring assistance the door is very heavy, and forcing it can damage the door or cause injury. Call a technician instead.

How long does a garage door repair take?

Many repairs take one to two hours. Spring and cable work is often finished in a single visit.

How often should my garage door be serviced?

Once a year is a good rule for most homes. Doors used heavily may benefit from a check more often.

What hours do you offer residential service?

Residential hours are 9 AM to 6 PM, Monday to Saturday. Same-day appointments are often available depending on the schedule. Commercial service runs 24/7.
